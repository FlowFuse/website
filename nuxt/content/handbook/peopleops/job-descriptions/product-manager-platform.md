# Product Manager - Platform

## Job Description

The Product Manager - Platform owns the FlowFuse platform outright: the solution-agnostic foundation underneath every customer solution. That scope covers enterprise management, edge and cloud deployment, industrial connectivity, industrial data modeling and contextualization, security, operator experience, and the reusable AI/ML capabilities that differentiate FlowFuse.

This role sets the direction for the platform over the next several years and writes the GitHub issues engineers build from. The work is hands-on and often unglamorous: exacting, technical tickets, not roadmaps from 30,000 feet. FlowFuse runs no classic PM / designer / engineer trio. It is mostly the PM and the engineers.

The [Lead Product Manager](/handbook/peopleops/job-descriptions/lead-product-manager/) role applies the same shape to other areas of the product.

The Product Manager - Platform is primarily responsible for:

* **Owning the platform roadmap.** Hold the multi-year, solution-agnostic view, then break it into work that ships this sprint. Decide where to invest beyond today's customer requests, and what to build, integrate, or source through partners. Track where industrial software and our competitors are heading, and decide where FlowFuse can establish meaningful differentiation.
* **Running the field-to-platform loop.** Partner with Solutions Engineering to spot product gaps and recurring patterns in customer deployments. Decide what becomes a platform capability, what stays in the solution library, and what remains customer-specific. The goal: field-developed work becomes repeatable enterprise solutions, not unicorns the team rebuilds for every customer.
* **Running continuous discovery.** Sit in on sales and customer calls, mine the call recordings for the problem behind the request, and talk to users directly. Learn FlowFuse customers and the industrial IoT (IIoT) domain fast, then keep learning.
* **Making industrial data a first-class part of the platform.** Lead the direction for industrial data modeling and contextualization: data models the platform defines once and enforces across instances and sites, and the relationships between machines, processes, production events, decisions, and outcomes that our workflows and AI build on. AI readiness starts here.
* **Shaping AI/ML in the platform.** Lead the product direction for reusable AI/ML capabilities, including AI-assisted development, agentic workflows, industrial data reasoning, and machine vision integration. Judge clearly what is production-ready and what is a promising demo.
* **Making the platform enterprise-ready.** Make it easier for customers and solution engineers to build, deploy, govern, and operate distributed Node-RED applications across many sites, mixed equipment, and cloud or self-hosted environments.
* **Running the lane day to day.** Triage the backlog and every new idea that flies in, prioritize ruthlessly, sequence the work, and move it into sprints.
* **Writing exacting specifications.** Write precise, technical, unambiguous GitHub issues with testable acceptance criteria. Engineers should never have to guess what "done" means.
* **Running refinement with engineering.** Show up prepared, make the product calls, unblock the team fast, and validate outcomes after release.
* **Filtering and translating input.** Leadership moves fast and thinks out loud. Ideas arrive at high volume and high velocity from the CEO, our investors, peers, and the live customer deals sales is racing to close. Absorb that input, turn it into a clear and sequenced plan, and protect the engineering team's focus. Stop the chaos at this desk instead of passing it through.
* **Wearing other hats.** FlowFuse is a small startup. Some weeks the highest-leverage work sits outside this job description. Do it anyway.

The Product Manager - Platform reports to the VP of Engineering and Product.

### Key relationships

* **Solutions Engineering** own customer solutions, industry-specific applications, and solution packaging, and they are this role's most important internal customer. Turn their field requests into platform capabilities that serve many solutions, not one, without giving the team whiplash.
* **The CTO** partners on technical direction and the Node-RED ecosystem, and leads the Node-RED open-source project. This role can advocate for upstream changes, but does not own that project.

### Where this role goes

The Product Manager - Platform starts as a product team of one, owning the platform and doing the groundwork directly. The role may stay a team of one for a while, and we say that up front. From day one it brings a point of view on where the platform should go; we hire someone to shape the plan, not only to execute an inherited one.

Run it well and the path opens: take on more of the product surface, build and lead a product team, and help shape the wider product vision for FlowFuse. That scope adds to the role rather than replacing it. A bigger title does not change the baseline of doing the work directly, at least not until the PM has built a team.

We hire for the right person here, not a fixed title. A strong mid-level PM ready to own an area, a senior PM who wants back into the hands-on craft, or someone aiming at Director all start in the same seat: owning an area and doing the work themselves. That baseline holds regardless of experience, and it is non-negotiable.

### What is the Product Manager - Platform not responsible for?

- People management. This role leads product decisions and runs refinement, but does not manage the engineers implementing the work. Building and managing a product team is a growth path for the role, not part of the starting scope.
- Technical decisions on how the platform is built or architected. Engineering owns the how, the technical design, and delivery estimates. Product and engineering make scope and sequencing tradeoffs together.
- Shipping on time. Engineering owns delivery.
- The Node-RED open-source project, which the CTO leads.
- Customer solutions, industry-specific applications, and solution packaging, which Solutions Engineering owns.
- Effort estimation. The Product Manager - Platform supplies effort estimates when creating issues, but the CTO is the DRI for that process.

## Skills

What the Product Manager - Platform brings to the table:

* **Bias Toward Action:** Moves fast, ships, and shows the work. Makes a reversible call today instead of scheduling a meeting about it next week.
* **Proven Craft:** Has owned a backlog, written engineering-ready specifications and tickets, and shipped capabilities that customers adopted. We ask to see it.
* **Strategic Product Vision and Competitive Foresight:** Looks three to five years out and explains where industrial software, data, and AI are heading, what competitors are building, and what that means for what we build, partner for, or avoid.
* **Industrial Depth, or Proof of Learning Hard Domains Fast:** Experience in manufacturing, industrial automation, or IIoT, and an understanding of how OT, plant-floor systems, and enterprise IT intersect, counts for a lot. So does a track record of going deep on something hard, with receipts.
* **Platform Experience:** Has shipped B2B platforms, infrastructure, developer tools, or similarly technical products, and has found reusable capabilities inside customer-specific work.
* **Fluency with Engineers:** Speaks their language, respects their constraints, and makes their work easier. This role earns the engineering team's trust, or it does not work.
* **AI/ML Product Judgment:** Has taken AI/ML or LLM-powered capabilities from concept to adoption, ideally at the edge or in industrial settings. At minimum, tells a production-ready capability from a promising demo.
* **Fluency with AI Tools:** Already uses AI tools to draft specifications, prototype, and move faster, and wants better ways to work.
* **Spine:** Coaches people toward their best work, holds a firm line on guardrails, and makes the hard call when someone will not improve. Tough and fair.
* **A Taste for Startups:** Thrives on ambiguity rather than predictability, and picks up the highest-leverage work even when it sits outside the job description.

People management experience helps, but the role does not require it to start. Familiarity with Node-RED or open-source ecosystems helps too.

## 90 Day Plan

* **Day 30:** Knows the platform, its backlog, and FlowFuse's biggest customer commitments. Works daily in the tools the team runs on (GitHub, Slack, Asana, Claude, Google Workspace). Has sat in on refinements and written tickets to the standard we expect. Has met Solutions Engineering, sales, and marketing, and knows who owns what.
* **Day 60:** Owns the platform backlog and publishes a prioritized roadmap. Writes tickets their engineers trust enough to stop asking what they meant. Has stood up the field-to-platform intake process with Solutions Engineering and made real calls on what becomes platform and what does not.
* **Day 90:** Has shipped a multi-week platform effort that solved a real customer problem and that more than one deployment reuses. Has set baselines for reuse, time-to-value, adoption, and reliability, and picked the first one or two AI/ML opportunities with a validation plan. Is the person everyone goes to for the state of the platform.

### First year

* Ships platform capabilities that multiple customer deployments reuse, instead of rebuilding them in the field.
* Turns field-developed solutions into repeatable enterprise offerings.
* Measurably cuts onboarding effort, deployment effort, and solution delivery time against the 90-day baselines.
* Holds a credible long-term platform strategy alongside an always execution-ready near-term roadmap.

## Hiring Plan

The interview process mirrors how FlowFuse hires engineers: it weights demonstrated ability over polished interview answers. Every stage answers one question: can this person own the FlowFuse platform independently and become a trusted partner to engineering?

1. Application Review: Candidates skip the cover letter and send one real ticket or issue they wrote, exactly as the engineer received it. They redact anything confidential but do not clean it up or reconstruct it from memory. They add two or three sentences on why they picked it, spending most of those sentences on what they would do differently now. The hiring manager reviews the resume and the work sample.
1. [Screening Call](/handbook/peopleops/hiring/screening-call/) (20m): The hiring manager or People Ops covers role fit, motivation, remote and async experience, current AI workflow, and alignment with how FlowFuse works.
1. Hiring Manager Interview (45m): The hiring manager digs into past product work, product judgment, and a realistic FlowFuse scenario. This stage gates for core product management craft: ownership, prioritization, discovery, and the ability to protect engineering focus.
1. Skills Assessment with the CTO and an Engineer (45m): The CTO and one engineer work through the candidate's submitted ticket, including refinement-style pushback of the kind engineers raise every day, then a shared FlowFuse product scenario we give every candidate for comparability. The conversation covers how the candidate works with engineering day to day, how they handle disagreement over scope and sequencing, and what they expect from the people building their tickets, rather than grading the artifact itself. The engineer completes a scorecard and we expect them to raise concerns. This stage also indicates what level the candidate would suit.
1. Platform Strategy Exercise: The candidate presents how they would approach FlowFuse's platform strategy over the next three years: what they would build, what they would partner for, what they would avoid, and where FlowFuse could establish meaningful differentiation. The ticket shows us how a candidate executes; this shows us how they think about where the market is going.
1. Team Interview (45m): Two team members not already in the interview loop cover team collaboration, communication style, and culture fit. They complete a scorecard and we expect them to raise concerns.
1. Offer: The hiring manager extends an offer to the selected candidate.
