# Senior Product Manager - Platform

## Job Description

The Senior Product Manager - Platform owns the FlowFuse platform outright: the solution-agnostic foundation every customer solution is built on. That scope covers enterprise management, edge and cloud deployment, industrial connectivity, industrial data modeling and contextualization, security, operator experience, and the reusable AI/ML capabilities that differentiate FlowFuse.

This role builds on the [Product Manager](/handbook/peopleops/job-descriptions/product-manager/) role and shares its shape with the [Lead Product Manager](/handbook/peopleops/job-descriptions/lead-product-manager/): a single owner who lives in their domain and runs it end to end. The distinguishing scope here is the platform itself, and the multi-year view of where it goes.

The work is hands-on. The role sets the direction for the platform over the next several years and writes the GitHub issues engineers build from. Tickets are expected to be exacting and technical. There is no classic PM / designer / engineer trio at FlowFuse; it is mostly the PM and the engineers.

The Senior Product Manager - Platform is primarily responsible for:

* **Owning the platform roadmap.** Holding the multi-year, solution-agnostic view, then breaking it into work that ships this sprint. Deciding where to invest beyond today's customer requests, and what to build, integrate, or source through partners. Tracking where industrial software and competitors are heading, and deciding where FlowFuse can establish meaningful differentiation.
* **Running the field-to-platform loop.** Partnering with Solutions Engineering to spot product gaps and recurring patterns in customer deployments, and deciding what becomes a platform capability, what stays in the solution library, and what remains customer-specific. The goal is that field-developed work becomes repeatable enterprise solutions rather than unicorns rebuilt for every customer.
* **Continuous discovery.** Sitting in on sales and customer calls, mining call recordings for the problem behind the request, and talking to users directly. Developing deep knowledge of FlowFuse customers and the industrial IoT (IIoT) domain quickly, and keeping it current.
* **Making industrial data a first-class part of the platform.** Leading the direction for industrial data modeling and contextualization: data models defined once and enforced across instances and sites, and the relationships between machines, processes, production events, decisions, and outcomes that workflows and AI build on. AI readiness starts here.
* **Shaping AI/ML in the platform.** Leading the product direction for reusable AI/ML capabilities (AI-assisted development, agentic workflows, industrial data reasoning, machine vision integration), with clear judgment about what is production-ready versus a promising demo.
* **Making the platform enterprise-ready.** Making it easier for customers and solution engineers to build, deploy, govern, and operate distributed Node-RED applications across many sites, mixed equipment, and cloud or self-hosted environments.
* **Running the lane day to day.** Triaging the backlog and incoming ideas, prioritizing, sequencing, and moving work into sprints.
* **Writing exacting specifications.** Precise, technical, unambiguous GitHub issues with testable acceptance criteria. Engineers should never have to guess what "done" means.
* **Running refinement with engineering.** Arriving prepared, making product calls, unblocking quickly, and validating outcomes after release.
* **Filtering and translating input.** Leadership moves fast and thinks out loud. Ideas arrive at high volume and high velocity, from the CEO, investors, peers, and the live customer deals sales is racing to close. The PM absorbs that input, turns it into a clear and sequenced plan, and protects the engineering team's focus. Chaos stops at this desk rather than being passed through.

The Senior Product Manager - Platform reports to the VP of Engineering and Product.

### Key relationships

* **Solutions Engineering** own customer solutions, industry-specific applications, and solution packaging. They are this role's most important internal customer. Being effective means turning their field requests into platform capabilities that serve many solutions, not one, without the team feeling whiplash.
* **The CTO** is a partner on technical direction and the Node-RED ecosystem, and leads the Node-RED open-source project. This role can advocate for upstream changes, but does not own that project.

### Where this role goes

This starts as a product team of one, owning the platform and doing the groundwork directly. It may stay a team of one for a while, and we say that up front. From day one, the expectation is a point of view on where the platform should go; we are hiring someone to shape the plan, not only execute an inherited one.

Proven in the seat, the path opens: taking on more of the product surface, building and leading a product team, and helping shape the wider product vision for FlowFuse. That scope is additive, not a graduation. A bigger title does not change the baseline of doing the work directly, at least not until a team has been built.

We hire for the right person here rather than a fixed title. A strong mid-level PM ready to own an area, a senior PM who wants back into the hands-on craft, or someone whose sights are set on Director all start in the same seat: owning an area and doing the work themselves. Experienced or not, that is the baseline, and it is non-negotiable.

### What is the Senior Product Manager - Platform not responsible for?

The base [Product Manager](/handbook/peopleops/job-descriptions/product-manager/) exclusions apply, with the following clarifications:

- People management: this role leads product decisions and runs refinement, but does not manage the engineers implementing the work. Building and managing a product team is a growth path for the role, not part of the starting scope.
- Technical decisions on how the platform is built or architected. Engineering owns the how, technical design, and delivery estimates. Scope and sequencing tradeoffs are made together.
- The Node-RED open-source project, which the CTO leads.
- Customer solutions, industry-specific applications, and solution packaging, which Solutions Engineering owns.
- Effort estimations. The Senior Product Manager - Platform provides effort estimation when creating issues. However, the DRI for this process is the CTO.

## Skills

What the Senior Product Manager - Platform brings to the table:

* **Bias Toward Action:** Moves fast, ships, and shows their work. Prefers making a reversible call today over scheduling a meeting about it next week.
* **Proven Craft:** Has owned a backlog, written engineering-ready specifications and tickets, and shipped capabilities that got adopted. We ask to see it.
* **Strategic Product Vision and Competitive Foresight:** Can look three to five years out and explain where industrial software, data, and AI are heading, what competitors are building, and what that means for what we build, partner for, or avoid.
* **Industrial Depth, or Proof of Learning Hard Domains Fast:** Experience in manufacturing, industrial automation, or IIoT, and an understanding of how OT, plant-floor systems, and enterprise IT intersect, is a strong plus. So is a track record of going deep on something hard, with receipts.
* **Platform Experience:** Has shipped B2B platforms, infrastructure, developer tools, or similarly technical products, and has found reusable capabilities inside customer-specific work.
* **Fluency with Engineers:** Speaks their language, respects their constraints, and makes their work easier. Earning the engineering team's trust is core to the role.
* **AI/ML Product Judgment:** Experience taking AI/ML or LLM-powered capabilities from concept to adoption, ideally at the edge or in industrial settings, is a strong plus. At minimum, can tell a production-ready capability from a promising demo.
* **Fluency with AI Tools:** Already uses AI tools to draft specifications, prototype, and work faster, and is hungry for better ways to work.
* **Spine:** Coaches people toward their best work, holds a firm line on guardrails, and makes the hard call when someone will not improve. Tough and fair.
* **A Taste for Startups:** Thrives on ambiguity rather than predictability, and picks up the highest-leverage work even when it sits outside the job description.

People management experience is welcome but not required to start. Familiarity with Node-RED or open-source ecosystems is a plus.

## 90 Day Plan

* **Day 30:** Knows the platform, its backlog, and FlowFuse's biggest customer commitments. Fluent in the tools the team runs on (GitHub, Slack, Asana, Claude, Google Workspace) and working in them daily. Has sat in on refinements and written tickets to the expected standard. Has met Solutions Engineering, sales, and marketing, and knows who owns what.
* **Day 60:** Owns the platform backlog and a published, prioritized roadmap. Their engineers trust their tickets enough to stop asking what they meant. Has stood up the field-to-platform intake process with Solutions Engineering and made real calls on what becomes platform and what does not.
* **Day 90:** Has shipped a multi-week platform effort that solved a real customer problem and is reused beyond one deployment. Has set baselines for reuse, time-to-value, adoption, and reliability, and picked the first one or two AI/ML opportunities with a validation plan. Is the go-to person across the organization for the state of the platform.

### First year

* Platform capabilities shipped and reused across multiple customer deployments, not rebuilt in the field.
* Field-developed solutions turned into repeatable enterprise offerings.
* Measurable reductions in onboarding effort, deployment effort, and solution delivery time against the 90-day baselines.
* A credible long-term platform strategy alongside an always execution-ready near-term roadmap.

## Hiring Plan

The interview process mirrors how FlowFuse hires engineers: it prioritizes demonstrated ability over polished interview answers. Every stage answers one question: can this person independently own the FlowFuse platform and become a trusted partner to engineering?

1. Application Review: No cover letter. Candidates submit one real ticket or issue they wrote, exactly as the engineer received it, redacted but not cleaned up or reconstructed from memory, plus two or three sentences on why they picked it, weighted toward what they would do differently now. Done by the hiring manager.
1. [Screening Call](/handbook/peopleops/hiring/screening-call/) (20m): Initial screener focused on role fit, motivation, remote and async experience, current AI workflow, and alignment with how FlowFuse works. Conducted by the hiring manager or People Ops.
1. Hiring Manager Interview (45m): A deeper conversation covering past product work, product judgment, and a realistic FlowFuse scenario. This stage gates for core product management craft: ownership, prioritization, discovery, and the ability to protect engineering focus. Conducted by the hiring manager.
1. Skills Assessment (45m): The candidate's submitted ticket anchors the conversation, including refinement-style pushback of the kind engineers raise every day. The discussion focuses on day-to-day working style, decision-making under pressure, and how the candidate interacts with engineering, rather than grading the artifact itself. Conducted by the CTO and a product peer. This stage also indicates what level the candidate would suit.
1. Platform Strategy Exercise: The candidate presents how they would approach FlowFuse's platform strategy over the next three years: what they would build, what they would partner for, what they would avoid, and where FlowFuse could establish meaningful differentiation. The ticket shows how a candidate executes; this shows how they think about where the market is going.
1. [STAR Interview](/handbook/peopleops/hiring/star-questions/) (45m): A behavioral interview to understand past experiences and assess alignment with FlowFuse's values, initiative, and collaboration style. Conducted by the CEO.
1. Team Interview (45m): Conversation focused on team collaboration, communication style, and culture fit. Conducted by two team members not already in the interview loop, who complete a scorecard and are expected to raise concerns.
1. Offer: Extend an offer to the selected candidate.
